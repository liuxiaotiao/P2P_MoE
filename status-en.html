01The idea, in one screen

Split the model by layer. The front half is task-agnostic; the back half is task-specific and only resident for the experts that task actually uses.

Front — task-agnostic, union of experts Back — per-task, hot experts only Standby front L₀ = 11

A request lands on any front chain. While computing layers 1–11 the front identifies the task online from its own routing histogram, then dispatches to a back chain for that task. Seven chains across 15 nodes: three fronts plus one standby, two mbpp backs and one gsm8k back.

The whole pool pulls less than one full copy. 141 GB against a 160 GB model — because every front holds only 11 of 48 layers, and every back keeps only the experts covering 70% of activation mass. The heaviest single node carries 22.4 GB; the lightest, 0.9 GB.

Capabilities that carry the design

Tensor-level fetch

Read a safetensors header, compute which byte ranges hold the wanted tensors, pull only those. 141 GB across the pool instead of 2,400 GB.

Partial expert residency

A layer can hold any subset of its experts. Three policies when a routed expert is absent: drop with gate renormalization, drop without, or re-take top-k among residents.

Online task identification

The front classifies from its own routing histogram against per-task activation profiles — no separate classifier model, no extra forward pass.

Blind binding

An arriving request takes the queue-head front with no comparison at all. Identification happens during the front pass; dispatch waits until it commits.

Relay for NAT'd nodes

Parked outbound connections spliced byte-for-byte. Neither side needs an inbound port, and the layer above cannot tell a relay is in the path.

Per-request timing

Compute measured locally as durations only; network inferred as the residual. Fifteen machines have no synchronized clock, so cross-machine instants are never claimed.

03How it is verified

672 tests. The distribution says where the risk was judged to be.

The two strongest results

The execution layer matches the reference implementation element-wise. Qwen3-Next's forward pass — 36 Gated DeltaNet layers interleaved with 12 full-attention layers, an always-active shared expert, zero-centered RMSNorm, partial rotary RoPE — is compared against transformers layer by layer. Assertion threshold 1e-3; measured maximum absolute difference 1.7e-05 on prefill, identical token sequences through 24 decode steps, and splitting the model across segment boundaries changes nothing.

Partial residency is lossless, not approximate. When the resident set contains every expert the router actually selects, the output is bit-identical to the fully-loaded model — torch.equal, not a tolerance. The approximation only begins when a routed expert is genuinely absent, and that case is measured separately.

Scope of these numbers. Parity is established against synthetic checkpoints with real key naming and real layer structure — the mechanism is proven, the numerics of the actual 80B weights are not yet checked. End-to-end latency and compute utilization under real load are instrumented and reported by the tooling, but not yet measured on the pool.

04The planning run on real data

Per-expert activation counts for gsm8k, math and mbpp, plus a measured 15-node topology with bandwidths and propagation delays. Three findings, each of which changed the design.

Activation is far less concentrated than assumed

Design assumptionMeasured, at 90% coverage
Resident experts per back-segment layer 10–20% of the full set gsm8k 197/512 (38%), mbpp 224/512 (44%)

A granularity ratio of 51 means a single token touches only 2% of the experts — but aggregated over a dataset, the tail of hot experts is long. This one number decides every memory budget downstream: a 3:5 task mix needs 8 channels at 577 GB, and the pool holds 321 GB. The deployed configuration settles at 70% coverage, two mbpp chains and one gsm8k chain, with load at 0.94× capacity.

Task overlap has no inflection point

The intended way to pick L₀ was to find where per-task expert sets stop overlapping. They never do: layer-wise Jaccard oscillates between 0.36 and 0.65 across all 48 layers with no trend (0.603 at layer 0, 0.553 at layer 47, period roughly 12). L₀ is instead chosen by identifiability — Bhattacharyya distance between per-task routing distributions, bounded via Chernoff — which puts the knee at L₀ = 11 with p ≥ 97.69%.

Coverage and channel count are not monotone

Load-to-capacity is 0.94× at coverage 0.70, 1.25× at 0.60, 0.83× at 0.50. Segment composition is discrete and hop counts are integers, so the relationship is not smooth — configurations are worth enumerating rather than reasoning about.

One dataset is unusable. In math_expert_activation.csv, experts 0–9 — exactly top_k — carry non-zero counts but NaN mean weights across layers 3–47, accounting for 27–30% of counts in the affected layers. Treating them as zero would permanently exclude what may be the hottest experts. The gsm8k and mbpp collections are clean; the deployment uses only those two.
